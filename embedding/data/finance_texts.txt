Inflation occurs when the general price level of goods and services increases over time, reducing the purchasing power of money.
Central banks use monetary policy to control inflation by adjusting interest rates and managing the money supply.
An increase in consumer demand can cause demand-pull inflation when demand grows faster than the economy's ability to produce goods and services.
Cost-push inflation occurs when production costs increase, causing businesses to raise prices for consumers.
Interest rates represent the cost of borrowing money or the return earned on savings and investments.
A central bank may increase interest rates to reduce borrowing and spending, helping slow down inflation.
When interest rates decrease, borrowing becomes cheaper, which may encourage consumers and businesses to spend and invest.
Gross Domestic Product measures the total monetary value of final goods and services produced within a country during a specific period.
Economic growth occurs when a country's production of goods and services increases over time.
A recession is commonly associated with a significant decline in economic activity across the economy.
Unemployment measures the percentage of people in the labor force who are actively seeking work but cannot find employment.
Fiscal policy refers to government decisions about taxation and public spending to influence economic activity.
Expansionary fiscal policy involves increasing government spending or reducing taxes to stimulate economic growth.
Contractionary fiscal policy involves reducing government spending or increasing taxes to control inflation.
Monetary policy is managed by a country's central bank to influence money supply, credit conditions, and interest rates.
Commercial banks accept deposits from customers and provide loans to individuals and businesses.
A savings account allows customers to deposit money and earn interest while keeping their funds accessible.
A fixed deposit is a banking product in which money is deposited for a specified period in exchange for an agreed interest rate.
Compound interest is calculated on both the original principal and accumulated interest from previous periods.
Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount over a specified period.
A loan is money borrowed from a lender that must be repaid according to agreed terms and conditions.
A mortgage is a loan used to purchase real estate, with the property serving as collateral.
Credit risk is the possibility that a borrower will fail to meet their financial repayment obligations.
Liquidity refers to how easily an asset can be converted into cash without significantly affecting its market value.
Financial markets allow individuals and institutions to trade assets such as stocks, bonds, and currencies.
A stock represents ownership in a company and may provide returns through price appreciation and dividends.
A bond is a debt instrument through which an investor lends money to an issuer in exchange for interest payments.
Diversification reduces investment risk by spreading investments across different assets and industries.
A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors to purchase a diversified portfolio of financial assets.
Exchange rates represent the value of one currency relative to another currency.
Currency depreciation occurs when a currency loses value relative to other currencies under a floating exchange rate system.
Foreign direct investment involves investing in business operations or assets in another country with a lasting interest.
A trade deficit occurs when a country's imports exceed its exports during a given period.
A trade surplus occurs when a country's exports exceed its imports during a given period.
Public debt represents the total outstanding borrowing obligations of a government.
A budget deficit occurs when government expenditure exceeds government revenue during a specific period.
A budget surplus occurs when government revenue exceeds government expenditure.
The consumer price index measures changes in the prices of a basket of goods and services commonly purchased by households.
Producer price indices measure changes in prices received by domestic producers for their output.
Deflation is a sustained decrease in the general price level of goods and services.
Stagflation describes an economic situation involving high inflation, weak economic growth, and high unemployment.
